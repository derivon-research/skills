# Mindmap Object Documents

Read this reference before creating or revising any concept or derivation source
document. Object documents are teaching publications with distinct ownership,
source fidelity, and a checkable prose standard.

## Give each document one role

A concept document states one reusable understanding state or established claim.
Open with the concept and its category, delimit its scope, distinguish its nearest
neighbors, and use an example, counterexample, or contrast when that makes the
boundary easier to recognize. It may state a result. Incoming derivations own the
arguments that establish that result; link to those derivations instead of
repeating them.

A derivation document is one problem-led response in which the full tail set
jointly establishes one head. Expose a concrete **problem pressure** in the
current concepts, then perform one identifiable resolving argument, construction,
or reframing that depends on the full tail set and arrives at the head without
using the head as evidence.

Problem pressure may be:

- **historical**: documented people encountered or stated it;
- **logical**: it follows from the limits, conflict, or incompleteness of the
  current ideas;
- **pedagogical**: a learner needs an abstraction to discuss, compare, or transfer
  what they know.

Let the prose make this provenance unmistakable. Attribute historical pressure
to evidence. Mark an Agent-composed logical or pedagogical account as a
reconstruction so the reader cannot mistake it for an actor's recorded words.

Problem pressure remains inside its derivation by default. Promote it to a point
only when understanding the problem is independently learnable and reusable. The
same pressure may produce a response family with distinct heads. Give each
response its own derivation. A later viewpoint uses an earlier viewpoint as a
tail only when it actually summarizes, refines, rejects, or otherwise reasons
from it; chronology alone supplies no derivation.

When a concept is a named viewpoint, label it with the author or school and a
plain-language thesis, such as `Proclus: the fifth postulate should be proved`.
A person, school, or era by itself is not an understanding state.

## Let the teaching content choose its shape

Use the structure that teaches this object directly. A definition may begin with
an example, a theorem with its statement, a historical claim with a short
narrative, a mathematical derivation with a proof, and an engineering derivation
with constraints and failed attempts. Use headings when they improve navigation.
Include source scope and genuine uncertainty where they naturally support the
account; omit empty boilerplate sections.

Preserve source meaning. Reuse authorized wording when it fits the object.
Otherwise quote only what is needed and adapt faithfully with a concise source
location. Keep conflicting sources explicit. Chapter adjacency, citation,
chronology, and co-occurrence remain documentary context rather than derivation
semantics.

## Write plainspoken teaching prose

Write idiomatic prose in the publication language. Put the subject and main claim
early. Give each sentence one main teaching job: state, explain, contrast,
qualify, or connect. When a definition also needs a reason, consequence, or
caveat, continue in a new sentence. Use grammatically parallel wording for
parallel ideas.

Begin a definition with the concept and its category, then develop its basis:

> 第一原理是演绎科学不再继续证明的起点。古典传统认为，人能够直接把握它为真，并以它作为后续知识的根据。

After the semantic draft is complete, perform a **plainspoken pass**. For every
sentence, identify its subject, main claim, and teaching job. Rewrite until all
three are clear on the first reading. Split a definition from its reason,
consequence, or qualification when they compete for the same sentence. Then read
the paragraph as a whole and preserve a coherent progression rather than a list
of disconnected short statements.

## Meet the completion gates

A concept document is complete when:

- its opening identifies one understanding state for the target learner;
- its scope and boundary distinguish it from adjacent concepts;
- examples or contrasts needed for recognition are present;
- source scope and genuine uncertainty are findable;
- prerequisite-based justification lives in linked derivations;
- every sentence passes the plainspoken pass.

A derivation document is complete when:

- one concrete problem pressure and its provenance are clear;
- the problem and resolving move depend on the full tail set, so removing any
  tail breaks the account;
- one identifiable resolving move establishes the head;
- the account reaches the head without assuming it;
- source scope, genuine uncertainty, and the weight rationale are findable;
- every sentence passes the plainspoken pass.

These are semantic gates, not required section titles.

## Structural example

Suppose the current concepts include the classical ideal of self-evident first
principles, Euclid's postulate system, and the finite reach of geometric
intuition. One derivation can ask why the fifth postulate lacks the same apparent
self-evidence, explain Proclus's response that it should be demonstrated, and
establish the viewpoint `Proclus: the fifth postulate should be proved`. A
different documented response to the same pressure receives another derivation
and another head. Research the historical claims independently before publishing
them; the example demonstrates graph and document roles rather than serving as a
source.
